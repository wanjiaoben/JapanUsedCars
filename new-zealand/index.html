New Zealand import guide

Import a used car from Japan to New Zealand

To put a used Japanese vehicle on New Zealand roads, NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi lists five areas to comply with: the Clean Car Programme, vehicle standards, a border inspection, entry certification, and registration and licensing. The vehicle must also arrive clean under MPI biosecurity rules and go through a Customs entry. Okinawa Auto handles the Japan side: buying the vehicle to your requirements, the auction inspection sheet, the export certificate and shipping, quoted on CIF terms. New Zealand-side clearance, certification and registration stay with the buyer or a New Zealand agent.

What should you confirm before bidding?

Most problems with New Zealand compliance are visible on Japanese paperwork before the car is bought. Check these with your entry certifier while the vehicle is still at auction:

  • The export certificate will show a type designation number (TDN) and model code.
  • The industry model code starts with an applicable emissions code.
  • The model code has no Kai mark, which indicates modification.
  • The vehicle has electronic stability control where its class requires it.
  • The manufacture date meets the frontal impact rule for its class.

What is the New Zealand import process?

  • Check the Clean Car Programme and the vehicle standards that apply to the vehicle.
  • Have the vehicle border-inspected by an Approved Inspection Organisation, either in Japan or on arrival in New Zealand.
  • Meet MPI biosecurity requirements so the vehicle arrives clean.
  • Complete the Customs entry and pay the GST and any duties Customs collects.
  • Present the evidence to an entry certifier for entry certification.
  • Register and license the vehicle if it will be used on the road.

NZTA: importing a vehicle

Which Japanese documents does NZTA expect?

Passenger vehiclesAn export certificate from Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) showing a type designation number (TDN) and model code, or a completion inspection certificate.
Mini-vehicles, 660cc or lessAn export certificate from the Light Motor Vehicle Inspection Organisation.
Proof of ownershipOriginal documents showing previous registration and an ownership trail back to the last registered owner in Japan.
Fuel consumptionA fuel consumption certificate for the entry certifier, obtained through NZTA's online tool when the vehicle was previously registered in Japan and has an export or completion inspection certificate.
If evidence is missingA statement of compliance is needed where the certificates do not show the required information.

NZTA: used vehicles from Japan

How do the frontal impact dates work?

NZTA treats Japanese-market cars as complying with the frontal impact rule by manufacture date:

  • Mini-sized cars under 660cc: manufactured on or after 1 July 2000.
  • Other cars made in Japan for the Japanese market: manufactured on or after 1 January 1996.
  • Cars made outside Japan and accepted through Japan's approval process: manufactured on or after 1 April 1999.

Exemptions may apply to vehicles over 20 years old, special interest vehicles, and vehicles brought in by people emigrating or returning to New Zealand.

What about biosecurity and the border inspection?

Vehicles must arrive in New Zealand clean and free of biosecurity contamination. Vehicles exported from Japan must be managed through an MPI-approved biosecurity system, and may need offshore treatment for hitchhiking pest risk. NZTA notes that untreated vehicles may be denied landing in New Zealand.

The NZTA border inspection is carried out by an Approved Inspection Organisation, in Japan or in New Zealand. Tell us at inquiry stage that the vehicle is New Zealand-bound so these steps are planned before shipping.

What does a used car from Japan need to be registered in New Zealand?

NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi lists five areas: the Clean Car Programme, relevant vehicle standards, a border inspection, entry certification, and registration and licensing if the vehicle will be used on New Zealand roads. The vehicle must also arrive clean under MPI biosecurity rules and go through a Customs entry.

Which Japanese document does NZTA expect for a passenger car?

For passenger vehicles other than mini-vehicles, NZTA expects an export certificate issued by Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism showing a type designation number (TDN) and model code, or a completion inspection certificate. For mini-vehicles of 660cc or less, the export certificate comes from the Light Motor Vehicle Inspection Organisation.

How is emissions compliance shown for a used Japanese vehicle?

NZTA looks for an applicable emissions code at the start of the industry model code on the export certificate or completion inspection certificate. If that evidence is missing, a statement of compliance is needed. Ask your entry certifier to confirm the code for the exact vehicle before bidding.

Does the car need electronic stability control?

NZTA tells importers to check for electronic stability control (ESC) on light vehicles, and the requirement depends on vehicle class and date. For example, class MC vehicles entering on or after 1 March 2016 must be fitted with ESC. Confirm the rule for your vehicle class with an entry certifier before purchase.

What does the Kai mark on a Japanese model code mean?

A Kai mark after the model code indicates the vehicle has been modified and may not comply with the standards it was originally approved to. NZTA advises checking with an entry certifier before importing such a vehicle.
