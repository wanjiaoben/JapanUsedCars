How to read a Japanese car auction sheet

A Japanese auction sheet is a one-page inspection report written by the auction house's inspector before a car is sold. It gives an overall grade, an interior grade, the mileage, a body map marked with damage codes, and written notes. Read the three together: the grade tells you the general level, the body map tells you where the marks are, and the notes tell you what the grade does not. Every vehicle we source comes with its auction inspection sheet, available to buyers on request.

What do the overall grades mean?

SNearly new. Very recent model year and very low mileage, with no visible marks.
6 / 5Excellent. Low mileage and close to showroom condition, with at most very minor marks.
4.5Very good. A clean used car with only small marks that are hard to notice.
4Good. Small scratches or dents consistent with normal use.
3.5Average. Noticeable scratches, dents or paint wear, or some repair work likely to be needed.
3Below average. Heavy marks, larger dents, or panels that need paint or repair.
2 / 1Poor. Significant damage, corrosion, flood history or heavy modification, depending on the auction house.
R / RAAccident repair history. RA is generally a lighter repair, R a more significant one.

These are the typical meanings. Each auction house publishes and applies its own standards, so a grade 4 at one auction is not identical to a grade 4 at another. Use the grade as a first filter, not as the decision.

What do the interior grades mean?

  • A — clean, with no significant wear.
  • B — light wear or small marks; a normal used interior.
  • C — visible stains, cigarette burns, tears or worn trim that need cleaning or repair.
  • D — heavy wear, strong odour or damage.

Some auction houses also grade the exterior with a separate letter.

How do the body map codes work?

The body map is a small outline of the car. Each mark is a letter for the type of damage followed by a number for its size: 1 is small, 2 is medium, 3 is large.

  • A — scratch.
  • U — dent.
  • B — dent with a scratch.
  • W — wave in the panel, usually a sign of previous repair or repaint.
  • S — rust. C — corrosion.
  • P — paint fade or damage.
  • X — panel that needs replacing. XX — panel that has been replaced.
  • Y — crack. G — stone chip in glass.

What else is on the sheet?

  • Model code and chassis number — identifies the exact variant, which matters for import rules in your country.
  • First registration year — usually in Japanese eras. For Reiwa (R), add 2018: R3 is 2021. For Heisei (H), add 1988: H27 is 2015.
  • Mileage — in kilometres.
  • Shaken expiry — the date the Japanese roadworthiness inspection runs out.
  • Transmission and equipment — such as AT or MT, air conditioning, sunroof, leather and navigation.
  • Inspector notes — written in Japanese, and often the most useful part of the sheet.

What does the sheet not tell you?

An auction sheet is one inspector's assessment at the time of inspection. It is strong evidence of condition, but it is not a warranty and may not capture every mechanical issue.

It also says nothing about whether the vehicle can be imported and registered in your country. Age, emissions, steering side and approval rules are decided by the destination authority, so confirm local eligibility before purchase commitment.

If there is any item on a sheet you cannot read, ask us before bidding.

What is a Japanese car auction sheet?

It is a one-page inspection report prepared by the auction house's own inspector before a vehicle is sold. It records the overall grade, interior grade, mileage, equipment, a body map with damage codes and the inspector's written notes. Buyers outside Japan use it to judge condition without seeing the car.

What does grade 4 or 4.5 mean on a Japanese auction sheet?

Grade 4.5 usually means a very clean used car with only minor marks, and grade 4 means good condition with small scratches or dents consistent with normal use. Each auction house applies its own standards, so read the grade together with the body map and inspector notes.

What does grade R or RA mean?

R and RA indicate accident repair history. RA is generally used for lighter repairs and R for more significant structural repair, but the definitions differ between auction houses. Always check which parts were repaired or replaced before bidding on an R or RA vehicle.

What do A1, U2, W2 and XX mean on the body map?

The letter is the type of mark and the number is its size, from 1 for small to 3 for large. A is a scratch, U is a dent, W is a wave or sign of previous repair, and XX marks a panel that has been replaced. So A1 is a small scratch, U2 a medium dent and W2 a visible repair wave.

How do I convert the Japanese year on an auction sheet?

Auction sheets usually show the first registration year in Japanese eras. For Reiwa (R), add 2018: R3 is 2021. For Heisei (H), add 1988: H27 is 2015.

Is the auction sheet a guarantee of condition?

No. It is one inspector's assessment at the time of inspection. It does not replace destination-country approval, roadworthiness or registration checks, and it may not capture every mechanical issue. Treat it as strong evidence, not a warranty.

About this guide

Grade and code meanings are the typical conventions used across Japanese auction houses. Individual auction houses publish their own definitions, which take precedence for a specific vehicle.
